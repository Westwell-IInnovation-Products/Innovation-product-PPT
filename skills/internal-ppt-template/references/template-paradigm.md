# Internal PPT Template Paradigm

## Positioning

Internal decks should feel like a decision tool, not a brochure. Use restrained visual design, sharp hierarchy, and clear business language.

Core principles:

- Lead with the answer: every slide title states the message, not the topic.
- Show only the evidence needed for the audience to decide or align.
- Keep layout systems repeatable so teams can extend the deck without redesigning it.
- Prefer practical density: enough information for a meeting, not so much that the slide becomes a document page.

## Standard Deck Structure

Use this default structure unless the user provides a stronger one:

1. Cover: topic, owner/team, date, confidentiality level if needed.
2. Executive summary: 3-5 key points and required decisions.
3. Context: why this matters now.
4. Current state: facts, baseline, or problem diagnosis.
5. Insight: what changed, what was learned, or what is blocking progress.
6. Options or plan: recommended path and alternatives if relevant.
7. Impact: business value, user value, cost, risk, and measurable outcomes.
8. Roadmap or actions: owners, milestones, dependencies, and next review point.
9. Appendix: detailed data, assumptions, methodology, backup materials.

## Common Page Patterns

### Cover

Layout:
- Large concise title, 1-2 line subtitle, owner/team/date.
- Keep branding subtle. Avoid decorative hero imagery unless the internal brand system requires it.

Title pattern:
- `2026 Q2 Product Roadmap Review`
- `Customer Retention Diagnosis and Action Plan`

### Executive Summary

Layout:
- Left: 3-5 numbered conclusions.
- Right: decision required, key metric snapshot, or risk callout.

Rules:
- Put the recommendation in the first bullet.
- Use one line per point where possible.
- Avoid background detail; move evidence to later slides.

### Situation And Problem

Layout:
- Use a 2-column comparison, timeline, or issue tree.
- Highlight the gap between target and current state.

Good title:
- `Conversion loss is concentrated in onboarding step 2`

Weak title:
- `Problem background`

### Data Analysis

Layout:
- One primary chart per slide.
- Put the conclusion above the chart.
- Put definitions, sample period, and caveats in small footer text.

Chart rules:
- Use neutral colors for baseline series and one accent color for the key finding.
- Label directly where possible instead of relying on large legends.
- Do not use 3D charts, decorative shadows, or unnecessary gridlines.

### Comparison / Options

Layout:
- Table with criteria as rows and options as columns.
- Include a clear recommendation marker.

Criteria:
- Strategic fit, user value, business impact, cost, risk, dependencies, time to launch.

### Roadmap

Layout:
- Horizontal timeline by month/quarter or phased swimlanes.
- Show workstreams, milestones, dependencies, and decision checkpoints.

Rules:
- Distinguish committed, planned, and exploratory work.
- Avoid crowding every task into the timeline; summarize at milestone level.

### Project Review / Retrospective

Layout:
- Goal vs result, what happened, root cause, actions.
- Use a simple status system: on track, watch, blocked.

Rules:
- Separate facts from interpretation.
- Assign owners and dates to follow-up actions.

### Operating Metrics

Layout:
- KPI strip at top, trend chart in middle, diagnosis/actions at bottom.

Rules:
- Show target, actual, delta, and period.
- Use red/green only when the meaning is unambiguous and culturally appropriate.

### Closing / Decision

Layout:
- Restate recommendation.
- List decisions needed and immediate next steps.

Rules:
- Do not end with a generic "Thank you" slide unless requested.

## Visual System

### Canvas

- Default to 16:9 widescreen.
- Use a 12-column grid or simple left/right content grid.
- Keep consistent margins across slides.
- Use section divider slides sparingly.

### Typography

- Use a clean sans-serif font available in common office environments.
- Suggested hierarchy:
  - Slide title: 28-36 pt.
  - Section title: 24-30 pt.
  - Body: 14-18 pt.
  - Labels/footnotes: 9-11 pt.
- Keep title length under two lines.

### Color

Use a restrained corporate palette:

- Primary: near-black or deep neutral for text.
- Background: white or very light neutral.
- Secondary: cool gray for structure.
- Accent: one strong brand or business color for emphasis.
- Alert colors: red/amber/green only for status and risk.

Avoid:

- Large gradients.
- Decorative blobs.
- Too many accent colors.
- Heavy dark backgrounds unless the whole deck is intentionally executive-dark and readable.

### Components

Use repeatable components:

- KPI card: metric, value, delta, period.
- Status tag: on track, watch, blocked.
- Decision box: decision needed, owner, timing.
- Risk box: risk, impact, mitigation.
- Action table: action, owner, due date, status.
- Footnote area: source, time period, definitions.

### Tables

- Use tables for comparison and action tracking, not for raw data dumps.
- Keep borders light.
- Bold row/column headers.
- Align numbers right and text left.
- Use subtle row banding only when it improves scanning.

### Icons

- Use icons only to improve scanning of categories, statuses, or actions.
- Keep icon style consistent: stroke weight, size, and color.
- Do not use icons as decoration.

## Writing Rules

Use message titles:

- `Revenue growth slowed because new user conversion declined`
- `Option B delivers faster launch with acceptable operational risk`
- `Three dependencies must be resolved before pilot expansion`

Avoid topic titles:

- `Revenue`
- `Options`
- `Dependencies`

Use plain internal business language:

- Prefer `需要决策`, `当前判断`, `关键风险`, `下一步`.
- Avoid inflated wording like `全面赋能`, `极致体验`, `生态闭环` unless those are official company terms.

## Scenario-Specific Adjustments

Management update:
- Put decision needs and risks early.
- Keep backup detail in appendix.

Product proposal:
- Include user problem, business goal, solution scope, MVP boundary, metrics, risks, roadmap.

Data report:
- Lead with answer, then show metric movement, segmentation, root cause, action recommendation.

Project review:
- Show target vs actual, timeline variance, issue diagnosis, mitigation, owner/date.

Training:
- Use more examples and exercises, but keep the same visual system.
